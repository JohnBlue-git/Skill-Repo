# ASD-STE100 Writing Guidance

## Purpose and scope

ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) is a controlled-language standard for
technical documentation. It is most useful when readers need clear, consistent
instructions, such as in user guides, procedures, and troubleshooting steps.
It is not a universal style rule for every Markdown file.

Use this guidance as follows:

- **Apply ASD-STE100 when** writing user-facing technical instructions or when
  the user asks for it.
- **Use its clarity principles selectively** in README files, API documentation,
  and other mixed-purpose documents. Choose the level of control that fits the
  audience and content.
- **Do not impose it as a strict requirement** on design documents,
  architecture descriptions, specifications, or decision records. These can
  require precise terminology, alternatives, qualifications, and nuanced
  reasoning.
- **Follow the full standard when formal compliance is required.** Use the
  current official specification and its applicable rules; this short guide
  does not establish or certify compliance.

Markdown is only the document format. Decide how to write from the document's
purpose, audience, and any contractual or organizational requirements.

## Practical principles

When writing technical instructions:

- Use familiar, specific words. Prefer a direct verb over an abstract noun.
- Use one consistent term for each part, action, and concept. Do not vary a
  technical term just to avoid repetition.
- Prefer short, direct sentences and one main action per instruction.
- State who or what performs an action. Prefer active voice when it makes the
  actor clear.
- Use the imperative for steps that tell the reader to do something.
- Put steps in the order the reader must perform them.
- Make conditions, warnings, and required outcomes explicit. Do not remove
  information that a reader needs to act safely or correctly.
- Define abbreviations and unfamiliar terms when the audience needs them.
- Avoid idioms, slang, vague references, and unnecessary words.
- Check that each instruction is technically correct and can be followed as
  written.

These are practical writing principles, not a substitute for the controlled
vocabulary, grammar rules, or other requirements in the full standard. In
particular, do not claim compliance based only on short sentences or simple
words.

## Review checklist

Before publishing user instructions, check:

1. Is the intended reader clear?
2. Does each step tell the reader what to do and in what order?
3. Are terms used consistently and unambiguously?
4. Are conditions, warnings, and expected results included where needed?
5. Could a reader misunderstand or act on any sentence in more than one way?
6. If formal ASD-STE100 compliance is required, was the current full standard
   applied and reviewed?
